## Phase 1 — Finish playtesting (9 Nov → 20 Dec)

### Who

| Round | Teams | Setup | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Friends, observed | 2 | You watch silently, take notes, no help | Where people stall; how long each envelope takes |
| B. Blind, unobserved | 3+ | Box handed over (or mailed) with a feedback form; you are not there | It works without the designer — the real Kickstarter situation |
| C. Reset test | the same B teams | They repack with the repacking letter and pass it on | "Play it, repack it, lend it" is true (a selling point, see wording) |

Good sources for B teams: Ottawa escape-room staff and regulars, board-game cafés, r/escaperooms, and players of the free Hiking game on escapepack.ca.

### What to record per team (one sheet per team)

- Start and finish time; time per envelope (they write the time on each envelope as they open it).
- Hints used, and on which puzzle. Any puzzle where they opened the answer.
- Team size, experience (first escape game / many).
- Rating 1–10 for: fun, difficulty, "would you give this as a gift", "would you pay $___ for it" (open number).
- Best moment and worst moment, one line each.

### Exit targets (proposed; adjust after round A)

- 80%+ of teams finish (hints allowed).
- No single puzzle where more than half the teams need the full answer.
- No component damaged or lost in play or reset.
- A measured play time range to print on the box (do not guess it: the box and page must show real numbers).
- Watch the finale workload: if sorting 18 hop tickets + 22 postcards feels like admin, keep tickets grouped by journey (Q-063).

## Phase 2 — Costs, and cheaper parts (1 Nov → 15 Jan, parallel)

### 2a. What one box costs — fill these in

The full component list already exists: `Production/Norse_Parts_List.xlsx` (29 rows, no prices yet). Add five rows the sheet leaves out:

| Extra cost | How to get the number |
|---|---|
| Kitting (stuffing 15 envelopes + box) | Time yourself packing 3 boxes; × hourly rate |
| Mailer bag + label | Supplier listing price |
| Shipping per region | Weigh the full prototype; quote Canada Post, Chit Chats or Stallion Express (CA→US) |
| Kickstarter + payment fees | Budget 10% of funds (5% platform + about 3% + $0.20 per pledge processing, plus pledge manager) |
| Spares + damage | 5% extra print run |

**Only real price so far:** ImprintNow printed envelopes, CAD $1.35 each at 500, $1.30 at 1,000 (15 × 22 cm only; Q-052). With 15 envelopes per box that alone is about **$20 per box** — the single biggest cost to fix.

### 2b. Ballpark cost per box (unverified, for planning only)

| Group | Ballpark CAD at 500–1,000 | Biggest lever |
|---|---|---|
| Printed mailer box + bag | $4–7 | Box size is standard; white board, 1–2 colours outside |
| 15 envelopes + rune labels | $3–20 | Plain bulk envelopes + printed labels instead of custom-printed envelopes |
| Paper: 22 postcards, 4 maps, tickets, letters | $3–6 | One paper stock, ganged on few sheets, no hand trimming |
| Card parts: wheel, comb, tally, coins, ruler, hnefatafl | $2–8 | One punchboard sheet instead of six processes |
| Bought bits: magnifier, fastener, 23 pawns, medallion | $2–6 | Fresnel card; stock pawns; card or wood medallion |
| Kitting labour | $3–8 | Fewer loose pieces |
| **Total landed (before shipping)** | **about $17–55** | The wide range is why quotes come first |

**Real print quotes, 20 Oct 2026** (online quote system, each design ordered separately; Q-052): all printed cards and maps cost **$10.17 per box at 500** ($14.66 at 250, $7.87 at 1,000), above the $3–6 guessed for paper above. Postcards are about half of it. Next lever: ask for a ganged quote (several designs on one press run).

**Pricing rule of thumb:** pledge price ≈ 2.5–3 × landed cost per box, shipping charged separately. A box costing $20 lands at a $55–60 CAD pledge. Benchmarks seen: Escape Room in a Box ran a $45 USD tier; Envelescape (envelope escape game, CAD campaign) averaged $116 CAD per backer.

### 2c. Design changes that cut cost (proposals — each needs your OK and a test)

Ranked by savings. None changes a puzzle's answer.

1. **One die-cut sheet for every card part** (designer packs the boxes and punches the parts out, 20 Oct; so heavy die-cut card is enough, not game-grade punchboard). Cypher wheel disks + arm, Aud's comb, treasure tally, 12 coins, ruler and (optionally) hnefatafl pieces as round tokens, all on one or two sheets that players punch out. Replaces 3D printing, hand cutting and paper clips with one process. The tally needs a rivet like the wheel; coins as printed tokens keep their values.
2. **Envelopes — designer's direction, 20 Oct:** plain bulk string-and-button envelopes, stamped by hand with **3D-printed stamps**, one stamp per whole rune label (13 real + 2 decoys = 15 stamps). Get supplier quotes for plain kraft envelopes in the two sizes. Keep string-and-button: reuse is what makes lending work.
   - Model each stamp **mirrored** (several runes are not symmetric).
   - TPU (95A) face printed face-down on a smooth plate, PLA handle. Pigment ink; test on the real kraft, which bleeds.
   - A corner jig so every label lands in the same place. 15 × 500 boxes = 7,500 presses.
3. **Print the colour, not the paper.** Sage, coral, yellow, green and tan stocks are a home-printing choice. A print shop prints the background colour on one white stock at no extra cost, and one stock is cheaper to order and store.
4. **Standard sizes, few of them.** A printer cuts any rectangle, but standard sizes are cheap stock products (business cards, postcards, event tickets), match pre-perforated sheets for short runs, and fewer sizes mean fewer cuts. Current sizes read from the print PDFs (may include bleed):

   | Item | Now | Nearest standard | Change |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 22 postcards, Liv's memo, Liv's notes | 4 × 6 | Postcard 4 × 6 | none |

   **20 Oct update:** Liv's notes now include the approved help QR and code guide on the back. Quote 23 two-sided designs (22 postcards + Liv's notes) and 1 single-sided design (Liv's memo). The cost table and workbook Print quotes tab reflect this split; existing entered quote prices are preserved.
   | 14 rail, bus, ferry hop tickets | 3.5 × 1.75 | Business card 3.5 × 2 | +¼ in height |
   | 4 air hop tickets | 4 × 1.75 | Business card 3.5 × 2, or event ticket 5.5 × 2 | narrower, or longer |
   | Brattahlíð and Aud museum tickets | 3.5 × 6 | Postcard 4 × 6 | +½ in width |
   | Rouen museum ticket | 3.5 × 5.5 | Postcard 4 × 6, or quarter-Letter 4.25 × 5.5 (4 per sheet, no waste) | small |
   | Oslo hnefatafl ticket | about 2.5 × 5.1 | Event ticket 2 × 5.5 | check the text fits |
   | 3 transition receipts | about 5.4 × 2.5 | Event ticket 5.5 × 2 | check the text fits |
   | Plane ticket | about 7 × 2.9 | Large event ticket 8.5 × 2.75 | small |
   | Hnefatafl setup insert | about 4.75 × 7 | Card 5 × 7 | small |

   That leaves four sizes: 4 × 6, 3.5 × 2, 5.5 × 2 and 8.5 × 2.75 (plus 5 × 7). Every resize needs its own layout pass and paper test.
5. **Hnefatafl board printed on the inside of the box base**, or on the back of a sheet already in envelope 10. Removes a component. Check: is seeing the board from the start a spoiler or a teaser?
6. **Magnifier = flat Fresnel card** (credit-card size). Test it on the branch runes and the 1576 top edge first (PP-027).
7. **Medallion: card or wood token in the base box; struck metal coin as a stretch goal or deluxe tier.**
8. **Weight and thickness.** Shipping can cost more than the game. Weigh the prototype; if it is just over a postal weight step, trim paper weight to drop under it.

**Printing in Canada has an extra benefit:** US duties follow the country of origin, and China-made games have faced high US tariffs since 2025. Check the current rule for Canadian-made printed games before setting US shipping (see Admin below).

## How many copies to plan for (added 20 Oct 2026)

1,000 is the upside case, not the base case, for a first campaign with no existing audience. Comparable escape-in-a-box campaigns: A Haunting in the Highlands raised £5,217 (likely about 100–150 backers); Envelescape about 617 backers ($71,593 CAD ÷ $116 average).

| Scenario | Backers | Print run | Self-packing at 20 min/box (guess; time a real pack) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 300 | ~350 | ~115 h |
| Good | 600 | ~700 | ~230 h |
| Upside | 1,000 | ~1,150 | ~380 h |

- **Print run = backers + 10–20% late pledges + 5% spares + ~30 review/demo copies.**
- **Funding goal on the base case** (about 300 boxes), quotes at 500 and 1,000 (the parts list already has both columns).
- **Print after the campaign closes**, to the exact number: local digital printing has no 1,000-unit commitment.
- **Envelopes and mailer boxes are shared by every adventure** (backpack-kit Packaging), so buying 1,000+ of those is low risk.
- **The real ceiling is packing time**, not printing. Past ~600 boxes, plan for helpers or a fulfilment service.
- **Forecast from followers:** six weeks before launch, the follower count tells you which row you are in (about a third of pre-launch followers back).

## Phase 3 — Pre-launch (15 Jan → 1 Mar 2027)

### Steps in order

1. **Kickstarter account and project draft** (Canada, CAD). Decide the creator name: Escape Backpack or The Cryptic Bureau (the box cover says "The Cryptic Bureau"; open question).
2. **Pre-launch page live by 15 Jan.** Kickstarter's own data: 32% of pre-launch followers back, against 17% of people who arrive later. One big "Notify me on launch" button, launch date stated.
3. **One landing page on escapepack.ca** collecting emails, linked from:
   - the free Hiking online game (an end screen: "Liked this? Liv's Norse box launches 2 Mar"),
   - a **free demo puzzle**: the cypher wheel + L1 as an online toy or print-and-play PDF,
   - every playtester.
4. **Review copies out by 1 Feb**, so reviews land before launch: Room Escape Artist, The Logic Escapes Me, escape-room YouTubers and podcasts, board-game reviewers who cover puzzle games.
5. **List it on BoardGameGeek** (game page + designer diary) in February.
6. **Page and video finished by 20 Feb**; ask two people who never saw the game to read the page and say what it is in one sentence.

### Audience target

To fund on day 1 you need roughly: goal backers ÷ 0.32 followers. A 300-backer goal needs about 950 followers. Set the bar at 1,000 followers before launch.

## Phase 4 — The campaign (2 Mar → 23 Mar 2027)

### Settings

- **Length 21 days.** Launch a Tuesday, 10:00 ET. Ends before Easter.
- **Funding goal = the smallest print run that still works**, not the dream number. Example only: 300 boxes × $20 landed + 10% fees ≈ $6,600 → goal about $7,000 CAD. A small goal that funds in hours makes the "funded" banner do the selling.
- **Turn on Late Pledges before the campaign ends** — it cannot be enabled afterwards.
- **Regions at launch: Canada and US only.** EU/UK VAT and shipping come in a later wave or retail.
- **Charge shipping in the campaign**, by region, from real quotes.

### Reward tiers (keep it to 3 or 4)

| Tier | Contents | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Early raven (48 h, limited) | The box, small discount or a bonus postcard | Rewards day-1 backers; drives momentum |
| The Raven Inheritance | The box | The main tier |
| Two families | 2 boxes, small discount | Gift buyers; second copy to lend |
| Liv's patron (optional) | Box + metal medallion + name in Liv's acknowledgements | Higher pledge without changing the base box |

Avoid a digital-only tier until the digital game is finished (Q-051; physical game first).

### Stretch goals (only ones that cost little per box)

- Metal medallion for everyone (if the deluxe-tier price covers it).
- A bonus postcard or story extra in a decoy envelope (Q-050 option 3).
- Social goals ("1,000 backers → we publish Liv's travel playlist") — free.

### Daily rhythm

- Day 1: email list, everyone personally, every channel.
- Days 2–19: one update every 3–4 days (a behind-the-scenes photo, a prop close-up, a stretch goal). Cross-promote with 2–3 other tabletop campaigns live at the same time.
- Last 48 h: Kickstarter sends followers a reminder; send your own email too.

## Phase 5 — After funding (Apr → Jul 2027)

1. Pledge manager (Kickstarter's own, or Gamefound / PledgeBox / BackerKit) for addresses and add-ons.
2. Final print files: one documented build path (Q-063 warns old builders can overwrite approved output).
3. Print proof → approve → full run.
4. Kitting day(s) with a checklist (the repacking letter doubles as the packing checklist).
5. Ship, then one update per milestone. Deliver late June / July 2027.

## Wording that sells

### The one-liners (drafts to react to)

- **Title:** The Raven Inheritance
- **Subtitle (125 characters; keep under Kickstarter's 135):** "Cooperative escape game in a box. Follow Great-Aunt Liv's postcards across the Viking world and uncover your family's secret." Echoes the box cover line.
- **Hook sentence:** "Liv left you her field box: 22 postcards, 15 sealed envelopes and a rune wheel. Every code you solve tells you which envelope to open."

### Rules for the page

1. **Lead with the experience, not the parts list.** First screen: a hand turning the rune wheel and opening an envelope. Components come later.
2. **Say the facts players filter on, early:** players (2–5?), age (14+), play time (measured in Phase 1), one session or several, difficulty, "no spoilers on this page".
3. **Answer the one-shot objection head-on:** "Nothing is destroyed. Repack it in 10 minutes and lend it to friends." (only if the reset test proves it).
4. **Use concrete numbers over adjectives:** "13 puzzles, 4 Viking journeys, 18 real places" beats "an epic adventure".
5. **Show the real thing.** Photos of the printed prototype on a table; no renders where a photo will do.

### Words to use / avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| cooperative, escape game, puzzle adventure, rune, postcards, sealed envelopes | "revolutionary", "like nothing you've seen", vague "epic" |
| real Viking places, family secret, inheritance | Answer-revealing images (no postcard backs, no map routes) |
| play it, repack it, lend it | "Replayable" (it isn't; "shareable" is honest) |

## Admin and risks to clear before launch

| Item | Why it matters | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **AI-generated art** | Kickstarter requires creators to disclose AI-generated content; part of the tabletop audience is hostile to it. Stamps, ravens, logos, box art and ticket logos were made with ImageGen. | Decide: disclose plainly, or commission a human artist for the box cover and key art (add the cost). |
| **Image licences** | Some postcard fronts adapt CC BY / CC BY-SA photos (Q-009, Q-010). Commercial use is allowed; ShareAlike means those adapted images stay under the same licence. | Confirm every postcard image's source and licence before printing (the credits page is the record). |
| **Age rating** | Products for children 12 and under need third-party safety testing in the US (CPSIA) and must meet Canada's toy rules; coins and pawns are small parts. | Label 14+ and market as adult/family-with-teens. |
| **Name** | "The Raven Inheritance" may already be used. | Trademark and Kickstarter/BGG search. |
| **Business and tax** | Canadian sales tax (GST/HST) applies once sales pass $30,000 in four quarters; Kickstarter income is taxable. | Register the business; one hour with an accountant before launch. |
| **US duties** | Rules changed several times in 2025–26 (de minimis ended; tariff rulings). | Check current duty for Canadian-made printed games in January 2027 before fixing US shipping prices. |
