The logic

14 Oct 2026 (designer, box + envelopes, PR-007): no longer a puzzle: a worked example. The luggage tags are gone (PP-023 parked, Q-047 answered). Postcard L1, loose on top of the box with the rune wheel, tells the story and hands over the first code as an example of how the wheel works: “Set 1 0 2 1 on my rune wheel and open the envelope with the same runes!” The wheel's arm prints the three steps (PP-047), so the rule stays in front of players for every later code. Designer's brief in chat: the first note explains that the package is the adventure, how the cypher works and that it picks the next envelope (with an example leading to envelope 1), and the story; option 2 of three (story on L1, rule on the wheel) chosen. The tag text below is superseded.

Players have: Opening the box: postcard L1 and the rune cypher wheel lie loose on top of the envelopes. L1's message introduces Aunt Liv, the adventure and the four Norse figures, then says the Norse were felling trees at this spot in 1021 and asks players to set 1 0 2 1 on the wheel.

Players do: Turns the four rings until the left-hand windows read 1 0 2 1 (step 1 on the arm), reads the four runes in the right-hand windows (step 2) and finds the envelope with the same runes (step 3). This is the loop every later code uses.

They get: Code 1021 → runes ᚷᛉᚦᛉ → envelope 1: AD and Aud's comb (PR-007, reordered 15 Oct 2026; was L2, L3 and the magnifier).

Why it is there: The data is in plain sight on both tags; the only hidden thing is which tag goes first, and the postcard hands that over. It teaches the game’s core habit — the card carries the rule, the props carry the data — at its lightest.

  4. Hint 4

  5. Hint 5

Puzzle that opens envelope 4: What’s in the horn PZ-004decided

Uses: Postcard H2 · Staraya Ladoga · envelope 3 Postcard H3 · Kyiv · envelope 3 Postcard HD · Constantinople (decoy) · envelope 3

The logic

Three postcards each hide one attribute word behind a different riddle technique; only one drink fits all three.

Players have: Postcard H3 (Kyiv), postcard H2 (Staraya Ladoga) and postcard HD (Constantinople) — HD stops being a pure decoy and takes on this leg’s third lock, closing the “Constantinople still needs one assigned” gap in the [Final riddle tab].

Players do: Solve each card’s riddle for its one word — HONEY, DRINK, FERMENTED — using a different technique per card so no two feel the same, then combine the three attributes: the only drink that is honey, fermented and a drink is MEAD.

They get: Code MEAD. Opening this lock releases PP-013, PP-030, PP-031, PP-025.

Why it is there: Closes the last open lock of Harald’s leg (PZ-004) without touching the map, which the rune-and-perch branch already uses. Gives HD the real puzzle job every decoy is required to carry ([Final riddle tab]) instead of leaving Constantinople unassigned. H3 and HD are linked by real Rus’-Byzantine history, not only by this puzzle: Oleg of Novgorod made Kyiv the Rus’ capital and, per the Primary Chronicle, sailed on Constantinople in 907 — the treaty that followed gave Rus’ merchants trading rights there. That fact carries H3’s Fun Fact, deliberately not honey/bees, since the riddle itself already is the honey content and a Fun Fact repeating its own answer would break Q-015’s one-fact-per-voice rule. Superseded 26 Sept 2026: on review this was judged too close after all — same core claim (fortune made here funded the throne), just with dates added. HD’s Fun Fact is now unrelated Constantinople trivia (Varangian runic graffiti in the Hagia Sophia) instead.

Hints, easiest first
  1. Hint 1
    Three cards, three words, one drink.
  2. Hint 2
    What do you get when honey ferments?
  3. Hint 3
    Each card hides its word a different way — read one, count one, look at one.
Solution and code

Staraya Ladoga spells DRINK with the first letter of each of its five sentences. Kyiv’s riddle (sweet but not fruit, gold but not metal, made by thousands, six-sided walls) is HONEY. Constantinople’s picture puzzle (sugar feeding yeast, a bubbling flask) means FERMENTED. A fermented honey drink is MEAD.

Code: MEAD → wheel runes ᚱᛒᛚᚷ → envelope 4

Puzzle that opens envelope 5: The treasure route PZ-006decided

Uses: Postcard A2 · Hvammur · envelope 4 Aud's trail map (The Island Settlement) · envelope 4 Aud's Treasure Museum ticket · envelope 4

The logic

One portrait map, symbols not to scale, carrying line/area/point symbols instead of isolated icons — the split-panel design below was shelved 17 Sept 2026 in favour of this. Players walk the drawn path network and count what the route crosses.

Players have: Postcard A2 (Hvammur), Aud’s laminated journey map, and the Aud’s Treasure Museum ticket (six rows, one per leg, three tally columns: bridges, fords, gates).

Players do: Walk the six legs on the real drawn network, from the northern chapel to the wood beyond the ruin at I9. At the merged leg 5, follow the road to the correct “village at a crossing” (H9, not the lookalike near H3/G3) — A2’s message gives only the forest detail (“the road that followed the forest”) to tell them apart; it does not name a direction (corrected 17 Oct 2026, Q-063: the card never said “south”). Tally bridges, fords and gates crossed across all six legs.

They get: Code 532 (521 until the map redraw of 16 Oct 2026). Opening this lock releases PP-005, PP-010, PP-011.

Why it is there: Turns Aud’s own map from a final-route prop into a working treasure map without printing a second sheet, and makes the tally self-checking — a blue line or a gate is unmistakable, so counting is hard to get subtly wrong.

Hints, easiest first
  1. Hint 1
    The Island Settlement map and the Treasure Museum ticket go together: the ticket gives directions, and the Hvammur postcard says what to count on the way.
  2. Hint 2
    Start at the northernmost chapel on the map and follow the ticket’s six steps along the roads.
  3. Hint 3
    Keep three separate tallies as you go: bridges, fords and gates. On the fifth step, the postcard tells you which road to follow.
Solution and code

Six legs: chapel → chapel (1 bridge), → well (2 fords, 1 bridge), → landing (1 ford, 1 bridge), → standing stone (1 bridge), → village at the crossing, south along the forest road (2 gates, 1 bridge), → the wood beyond the ruin (nothing). Totals: 5 bridges, 3 fords, 2 gates: 532.

Uses: Postcard R2 · Rouen · envelope 8 Musée Ducal de Rouen ticket · envelope 8 Rollo's trail map (The Granted Lands) · envelope 8

The logic

Four English word pairs split along the Norman Conquest’s class line. The Rouen card fixes the order, the ticket turns pairs into coordinates, the map turns coordinates into a square-count.

Players have: Postcard R2 (Rouen), the Musée Ducal de Rouen ticket, and Rollo’s trail map with its word icons.

Players do: Read the four Saxon-register words out of the Rouen message in the order they appear. For each, find its Norman partner on the ticket — the Norman column gives a letter, the Saxon column a number, together a grid coordinate. Find that word’s icon already drawn on Rollo’s map, and count the squares between the icon and the coordinate. Four counts, one per dial of the lock.

They get: Code 1486. Opening this lock releases PP-007, PP-008, PP-009.

Why it is there: Gives Rollo’s map a second job besides the final route, and makes the Conquest’s language history the puzzle rather than the flavour. Swapping the directional lock for a numeric one keeps the same props, ticket and map work, just cheaper, more common hardware.

Hints, easiest first
  1. Hint 1
    Read the Rouen postcard next to the back of the Rouen museum ticket. Some of Liv’s words have a partner on the ticket.
  2. Hint 2
    The ticket pairs everyday Saxon words (numbered) with their Norman partners (lettered). A matched pair gives a letter and a number: a square on the Granted Lands map.
  3. Hint 3
    Liv mentions fighting, people, poultry and an inn, in that order. For each word, find its square on the map and count the squares to the little drawing for that word.
Solution and code

Fight: 1 square. People (folk): 4. Poultry (hen): 8. Inn: 6. In the postcard’s order the code is 1486.

Puzzle that opens envelope 11: The king’s unfinished escape PZ-008decided

Uses: Postcard H1 · Oslo · envelope 10 Hnefatafl board and pieces · envelope 10 Hnefatafl Museum ticket, Oslo · envelope 10 Harald's trail map (The Varangian Road) · envelope 10

The logic

Rebuild the board your aunt sketched on the back of a map, then finish the game she left set up.

Players have: The 11 × 11 board and pieces, the Hnefatafl Museum ticket with the rules, Harald’s Oslo map whose back carries the setup, and postcard H1 which sets the task.

Players do: Recover the setup from Liv's papers (the back of Harald's map and the museum ticket), place the pieces, then play the sequence: a dark piece moves, then the king, four times. The first two dark moves are the numbered arrows on the map back, the third is Liv's riddle, the last is marked by the shield icon on Harald's map. The king must reach a corner in exactly four straight-line moves. Count how far he travels on each leg.

They get: Code 253. Opening this lock releases PP-019, PP-020, PP-038.

Why it is there: The strongest physical centrepiece in the set, the only puzzle that can release something mechanically rather than through a dial, and the only one that gives a trail map a job before the endgame.

Hints, easiest first
  1. Hint 1
    The board is eleven columns wide. Count what you can actually see, and think about what the smudge hides.
  2. Hint 2
    Something was lying on the ink. It is still in the box, and it is the right shape.
  3. Hint 3
    Dark moves first, then the king, four times. The numbered arrows on the back of Harald's map are the first two dark moves; Liv's riddle gives the third.
  4. Hint 4
    The shield on the museum ticket is also printed on Harald's map. It marks where the last dark piece goes, and that move frees the way for the king.
  5. Hint 5
    The king does not have to run to the top of the column. Try stopping after one square.
  6. Hint 6
    F6 → H6 → H7 → K7 → K11, then measure each leg.
Solution and code

Set out the starting position from Liv's note, with columns I to K taken from the museum ticket. Then play: dark E1 → E6, king F6 → H6; dark H2 → H5, king H6 → H7; dark H10 → H11 (the attacker on the second-highest row steps up to the top row), king H7 → K7; dark K10 → I10 (the shield on Harald's map is at I10, and moving the piece on K10 frees column K), king K7 → K11. What to record per team (one sheet per team)

  • Start and finish time; time per envelope (they write the time on each envelope as they open it).
  • Hints used, and on which puzzle. Any puzzle where they opened the answer.
  • Team size, experience (first escape game / many).

Phase 4 — The campaign (2 Mar → 23 Mar 2027)

Settings

  • Length 21 days. Launch a Tuesday, 10:00 ET. Ends before Easter.
  • Funding goal = the smallest print run that still works, not the dream number. Example only: 300 boxes × $20 landed + 10% fees ≈ $6,600 → goal about $7,000 CAD. A small goal that funds in hours makes the "funded" banner do the selling.
  • Turn on Late Pledges before the campaign ends — it cannot be enabled afterwards.
  • Regions at launch: Canada and US only. EU/UK VAT and shipping come in a later wave or retail.

Wording that sells

The one-liners (drafts to react to)

  • Title: The Raven Inheritance
  • Subtitle (125 characters; keep under Kickstarter's 135): "Cooperative escape game in a box. Follow Great-Aunt Liv's postcards across the Viking world and uncover your family's secret." Echoes the box cover line.
  • Hook sentence: "Liv left you her field box: 22 postcards, 15 sealed envelopes and a rune wheel. Every code you solve tells you which envelope to open."

Rules for the page

  1. Lead with the experience, not the parts list. First screen: a hand turning the rune wheel and opening an envelope. Components come later.
  2. Say the facts players filter on, early: players (2–5?), age (14+), play time (measured in Phase 1), one session or several, difficulty, "no spoilers on this page".
  3. Answer the one-shot objection head-on: "Nothing is destroyed. Repack it in 10 minutes and lend it to friends." (only if the reset test proves it).
  4. Use concrete numbers over adjectives: "13 puzzles, 4 Viking journeys, 18 real places" beats "an epic adventure".
  5. Show the real thing. Photos of the printed prototype on a table; no renders where a photo will do.
