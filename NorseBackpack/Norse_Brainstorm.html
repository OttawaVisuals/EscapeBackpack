Suggested direction

A family secret, packed for a journey.

Your great-aunt has been tracing the family’s Nordic origins. She has found a connection to a dig in Norway, but her invitation is tucked inside a locked travel wallet.

Reconstruct her research, collect three pieces of a family medallion, and discover where she wants you to go.

Keep the original ending.

The prize is the plane ticket from your idea deck, plus a personal note: “I could use someone who notices what everyone else walks past.”

The ticket is a fictional game prop. The exact family connection and dig location are still open.

The reason for the locks

Your aunt loves setting family challenges. This parcel is her invitation test. Modern locks, labels and a website all make sense as things she prepared herself.

The recurring “aha”

The keepsake looks incomplete at the start. Each discovery repairs it. At the end, it becomes a tool that reads the field notebook you have had all along.

Why start this simple?

It teaches players to trust the supplied reference cards and gives access to the richer physical tasks. In the real bag, the key can be split across a catalogue card and the object tag.

This is her invented digit cipher using rune shapes. It is not a historical rune-to-number system.

The king’s escape

Move the king to a gold corner in exactly three moves. Slide any distance horizontally or vertically. Dark pieces stay fixed. No jumping or captures. Read the digit where each move ends.

A custom solo puzzle inspired by hnefatafl. These simplified rules are printed on the prop. No knowledge of the full game required.

Decisions for the next draft

Make the bag feel like it belongs to her.

Core physical kit

  • A field bag with clearly marked locked pouches.
  • Short letter, three postcards and a reusable notebook.
  • Replica object labels and small family record cards.
  • A folding 7 × 7 board with one movable king. Print the fixed pieces for easy reset.
  • Three medallion sections, a keyed viewing mask and a reading panel.
  • Saga strips, a travel wallet and a fictional boarding pass.

Bag, lock inventory, materials and dimensions are unconfirmed.

An optional website with a clear job

“Aunt’s Field Archive” holds her welcome message, three levels of hints per puzzle, and a closing message after the wallet opens.

Let physical locks control progress. The website adds atmosphere and help without becoming a second puzzle system. A printed hint booklet covers the same information.

Later option: the boarding pass opens an illustrated dig briefing. Audio always has a transcript. This page is the design guide, not that player website.

Other directions worth keeping

More magic: the sleeping raven

The keepsake awakens as the family story is reconstructed. A final message hints that the dig has uncovered something alive.

Keep the ticket reveal; make the supernatural layer a final tease.

More tactile: the tangled voyage

Replace the postcard opening with a short cord-routing puzzle around a stitched coastline. The correct path exposes three numbers through holes.

Prototype the cord and holes first. Wear and ambiguous routing could make reset harder.

More investigation: the wrong museum label

Compare a drawing, a repair note and a catalogue entry to identify a mislabeled replica. Its accession number opens the archive.

A swap for family records if you want less genealogy.

Three useful choices

  1. Tone: grounded mystery, subtle magic, or full mythic quest?
  2. Audience: children with adults, teens, or adults?
  3. Hero prop: a medallion assembled from pieces, a carved puzzle box, or a cloth map?

Working assumptions for this draft: a small mixed-age group, no prior Norse knowledge, a table available, and reusable props. No duration or difficulty claim yet.

Reset and prototype checks

Repack the wallet first, then WELL, then the two branch pouches. Put these and the ROOT piece in the archive. Return the rune materials to the main compartment and postcards to START. Scramble every lock. Keep a packing photo and a spare clue set.

Before final production: verify every digit is readable at print size; check the medallion fits only one way; verify that the mask reveals only the intended digits; check every clue has a matching pouch symbol; run an unfamiliar group through the paper version. Observe solve times before choosing a duration target.
